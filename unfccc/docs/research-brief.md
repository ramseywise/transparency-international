# Research brief: business and fossil fuel presence in country delegations at COP31

*Converted from `Research brief.docx` on 2026-11-24. The Word file is the original; the text is unchanged apart from formatting.*

## Purpose

This research should measure how much of the government-nominated presence at COP31 (UN climate conference 2026) comes from business, and how much of that is linked to the fossil fuel industry. Governments decide who gets the conference badges, with no UN eligibility rules or screening, so they are a key route for industry access to the negotiations.

Two badge types are participially relevant:

- **Party:** the official national delegation, normally officials, negotiators and ministers.
- **Party overflow:** extra badges a government hands out at its own discretion, often to business, NGOs, academics and guests. Holders get access to the Blue Zone (the official, UN-run conference area), and can observe negotiating rooms at times.

## Data

The source is the UNFCCC's COP31 list of registered on-site participants (Excel). It has one sheet per badge type. Only the **Parties** sheet (18,812 people) and the **Party overflow** sheet (15,997 people) are interesting for the research purpose. Affiliations are self-declared and free text. They are often misspelt, abbreviated, generic ("Private sector") or blank.

| Field | What it gives us |
|---|---|
| Nominated by | The country that issued the participant |
| Name, Functional title, Department | Who the person is and what they do |
| Home organization | Self-declared employer: the basis for every classification |
| Relation | How the person relates to the nominating government (e.g. paid contract, partner/sponsor, guest, unpaid adviser, chose not to disclose) |

## Question 1: How many delegates come from business, and from which sectors?

**For the EU and each of its 27 member states**, we want to count Party and Party overflow delegates whose home organisation is a business, and break them down by sector. Would be interesting to report Party and Party overflow separately, as a number and as a share of each delegation.

**Across all countries**, we could repeat the count if feasible. Which delegations have the most business representatives, and the highest share? With priority given to business on full Party badges, since those are seats inside the official delegation.

What counts as business: for-profit companies, industry and trade associations, consultancies, law firms, and financial institutions. Could be interesting to count state-owned enterprises separately rather than as public sector. Exclude NGOs, universities, think tanks and media.

## Question 2: How many fossil fuel-linked delegates did EU countries invite?

For the EU and each member state, count the Party and Party overflow delegates whose home organisation is linked to the fossil fuel economy. In our earlier work, we applied the following classification to each delegate's self-declared affiliation:

- **Tier 1 (direct fossil fuel interests):** oil, gas and petrochemical companies, as well as fossil fuel infrastructure operators.
- **Tier 2 (partial fossil fuel exposure):** utilities and companies operating in sectors with ongoing fossil fuel activities or dependencies.
- **Tier 3 (financial exposure):** banks, insurers and asset managers with documented fossil fuel financing exposure.
