# House style guide

## 1. Sources
Guide date: 2026-11-25. Derived only from these seven texts (short labels used below):
- **TF** ti-2025-trade-fair: web feature, 2025-10-15 (paragraphs).
- **DL** ti-2026-formal-delegations: web feature, 2026-09-14 (paragraphs).
- **BL** ti-2025-corruption-undermines-climate: news blog, 2025-02-11, 8 pages.
- **HIT** ti-2026-high-integrity-talks: policy paper, 10 pages.
- **FD** ti-2025-fuelling-delay: research report, 37 pages.
- **JT** ti-2024-jetps: research report, 42 pages.
- **DR** gverdtsiteli-maslen-undue-influence-draft: book chapter draft, unpublished (paragraphs).

All but TF and DL share an author with the lead researcher. A pattern is listed only if seen in two or more texts; otherwise it is marked "seen once".

## 2. Voice and stance
- **Person.** Features mix "we" and "Transparency International": "In collaboration with InfluenceMap, we examined 466 companies" (TF, para 9). Reports use no "we" and call themselves "This report": "This report focuses on the governance and corruption challenges" (JT, p.9; also FD, p.8). The chapter does both: "This chapter addresses this gap." (DR, para 9); "we examine how undue influence may occur" (para 32).
- **Attribution first.** "According to LobbyMap, both TotalEnergies and Siemens Energy have questioned" (DL, para 31); "According to the International Renewable Energy Agency (IRENA)" (JT, p.10).
- **Hedges on motive and effect.** "may", "reportedly", "allegedly", "arguably". "This has allegedly prompted internal debates" (FD, p.21); "Evidence shows that the damage caused by corruption to policies on climate may be long-lasting" (BL, p.4); "The lack of safeguards against disinformation arguably may further lend legitimacy" (DR, para 69).
- **Fairness lines.** Presence is not proof. "Their presence at COP doesn’t prove they swung a vote." (DL, para 9); "While a presence at COP alone may not demonstrate influence over specific negotiation outcomes" (DR, para 50); "it would be inaccurate to attribute the weak climate policies internationally solely to undue influence" (DR, para 78).
- **Contrast cases.** "JPMorgan presents a different profile." (DL, para 35); "In contrast, Brazil emphasised Indigenous leadership" (HIT, p.6).
- **Interviewees** get codes, never names: "(N1, N4, N5, N11, N14, N19)" (FD, p.16); "(Negotiator 1)" (DR, para 48).
- **Contractions** only in features and inside quotes.

## 3. Structure
- **Opening: setting, then the turn.** "Yet," opens TF para 3; "Yet none of this is possible without a strong foundation of integrity." (HIT, p.3); the JT summary opens on the excitement around JETPs, then "However, for JETPs to truly serve people and the planet, they must be safeguarded" (p.5).
- **Headings.** Reports and the policy paper: main sections in capitals ("WHAT IS A JUST ENERGY TRANSITION?", JT, p.10; "RECOMMENDATIONS", HIT, p.7), subsections in sentence case ("Methodology", JT, p.9; "Conflict of interest safeguards", HIT, p.7). Findings headings are full claims: "Insufficient transparency in JETP financing impairs public scrutiny" (JT, p.3); "Corruption stands in the way of effective climate action" (BL, p.4). Features and the chapter: sentence case (TF, DL, DR).
- **Paragraph length** (median words): TF 51, DL 57, FD 83.
- **Methodology states sources, counts and limits.** "nine semi-structured interviews – three per country" (JT, p.9); "39 semi-structured interviews conducted during spring-summer 2025" (DR, para 34). Limits are said outright: "the analysis was limited by the fact that no public officials were available for interview" (JT, p.9); "The sample may therefore reflect certain biases" (DR, para 35); "the findings should not be considered a comprehensive assessment" (DL, para 50).
- **Recommendations.** Reports: named actor plus "should", under capitalised theme headings. "JETP governing bodies should adopt a formal framework" (JT, p.7); "The UNFCCC should adopt clear, system-wide definitions" (FD, p.29); "The COP31 Presidency should issue a joint public commitment" (HIT, p.4). Policy paper and blog: imperative bullets. "Appoint an independent ethics adviser or panel" (HIT, p.7); "Eliminate loopholes that enable undue influence" (BL, p.8). "must" is kept for the strongest asks: "JETP governing bodies must facilitate early, meaningful and inclusive multi-stakeholder engagement" (JT, p.6).
- **Endings.** Features close on one urgency sentence: "As COP30 approaches, the need for stronger oversight of business participation has never been more urgent." (TF, para 42). Reports close on what the findings make possible: "The recommendations in this report chart a course for realising these goals" (JT, p.30).
- **Front matter** (reports, policy paper): author, reviewers, an accuracy disclaimer, ISBN, CC BY-ND licence (JT, p.2; HIT, p.2).

## 4. Numbers and evidence
- **Per cent.** Reports: "per cent" ("Only 26 per cent (US$17.9 billion)", JT, p.10; "Only 16 per cent of all meetings", FD, p.17). Features and blog: "%" ("88% of companies", TF, para 12; "around 40% of climate finance", BL, p.5). JT tables use "%" ("Around 4%", p.25).
- **Money.** "US$" and a figure: "approximately US$46.5 billion" (JT, p.5). A local amount is converted: "a billion rand (more than US$56 million)" (JT, p.16). Seen once.
- **Approximation.** "nearly", "around", "more than", "approximately": "nearly 60%" (DL, para 2); "approximately 90 per cent" (JT, p.16).
- **Small numbers and fractions in words:** "nine EU member state delegations" (DL, para 1); "nearly two-thirds of companies" (TF, para 5).
- **Citation by venue.** Features: hyperlinks. Reports and the policy paper: endnotes, Chicago notes style, with an article title in quotes: "Matteo Civillini, ‘PR Firm Working for Shell Wins COP30 Media Contract’, Climate Home News, 30 July 2025." (HIT, p.8). The chapter: author-date in the text, "(Chari et al., 2019, 4)" (DR, para 11), and a bibliography with "(accessed 9 October 2025)".
- **Captions.** "FIGURE 2." in capitals, then a sentence (DL; BL, p.6, with Source: and Note: lines below). "Box 1. Climate justice at the international level" (JT, p.11), seen once.

## 5. Terms and spelling
- **British spelling.** "analysed" (TF), "mobilising", "marginalisation" (JT, p.5), "decarbonisation" (DR, para 72). "-ize" only in proper names and quoted titles.
- **Party overflow.** Lower case "overflow" in TF, FD (p.10) and DR ("Party overflow badges", para 47); capital O only in DL. "Party" and "State Parties" (HIT, p.7) take a capital P.
- **Member State.** Mixed in DL (heading against para 1); lower case in DR ("member states", para 61).
- **COP30**, no space; plural "COPs". "Blue Zone" in capitals (DL, para 12; DR, para 46).
- **Acronyms.** Spelled out at first use, then the acronym: "International Labour Organization (ILO)" (JT, p.11). Reports also carry an abbreviations list (JT, p.4; FD, p.6).
- **Fixed phrases.** "undue influence", "net zero" (no hyphen), "fossil fuel-linked".
- **Dashes.** Spaced en dash for an aside: "the International Partners Group (IPG) – primarily composed of G7 countries –" (JT, p.5); "– such as energy production and distribution –" (BL, p.5); also DL, para 36. TF has a spaced hyphen once.
- **Serial comma.** Used in HIT ("transparent, predictable, and inclusive", p.4) and DR ("preconditions, techniques, and potential effects", para 9). Absent in JT ("transparency, accountability and integrity", p.9) and DL.

## 6. Do / avoid
| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Name the source before the claim or number (DL, JT) | Numbers with no stated source |
| Hedge motive and effect: "may", "reportedly" (FD, BL, DR) | Unhedged claims about intent |
| Add a fairness line: presence is not proof (DL, DR) | Implying attendance shows influence |
| Add a contrast case when criticising (DL, HIT) | One-sided lists of named bodies |
| State the method's limits outright (JT, DR, DL) | Hiding gaps in the sample |
| Report: named actor plus "should" (JT, FD, HIT) | Recommendations with no actor |
| Report: "This report", "per cent" (JT, FD) | "We", contractions, "%" in report prose |
| Spell out each acronym once (JT, FD) | Undefined acronyms |
| Give interviewees codes (FD, DR) | Names or job titles of interviewees |
| Feature: end on one urgency sentence (TF, DL) | A closing summary of every finding |

## 7. By document kind
- **Research proposal:** report register (Ramsey, 2026-11-24): impersonal, "This proposal", "per cent", endnotes, named actor plus "should".
- **Final online article:** feature register like TF and DL: "we" allowed, "%", hyperlinks, sentence-case headings, a closing Methodology section, an urgency ending.
- **Policy paper (HIT):** capitalised main headings, short "should" asks up front, imperative bullets under RECOMMENDATIONS, endnotes.
- **Research report (FD, JT):** front matter, abbreviations, executive summary with bulleted findings, Introduction with Methodology, claim headings, themed recommendations, endnotes.
- **Scholarly chapter (DR):** "This chapter" with some "we", author-date citations, research questions stated, limits of the sample discussed.
